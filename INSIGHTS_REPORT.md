# Guest Experience Insights Report: Melbourne short stays and the Crown precinct

**Prepared by:** Dhruv Rawat · **Data:** 377,310 guest reviews, Oct 2024 - Sep 2026 (Inside Airbnb, Melbourne) · **Audience:** Guest Experience and operations leaders

> This report uses public review data for Melbourne short-stay accommodation as a stand-in for a
> hotel's own Voice of Guest feedback. The method (sentiment, guest-journey themes, severity, key
> drivers, monthly scorecard) is the same one a GX team would apply to its own surveys, complaints and
> online reviews.

## Executive summary

1. **Star ratings hide the problems.** The average rating is 4.78 / 5 and only 1.5% of reviews are negative
   overall, but **5.9% of reviews contain a specific complaint.** Most complaints sit inside positive reviews
   ("great location, but the bed was awful"). A VoG program that tracks only the star rating would miss them.
2. **The Crown precinct is harder to get right.** Within 1.5 km of Crown, **7.0%** of reviews contain a
   complaint vs **5.3%** in the rest of Melbourne (**+31%**). The gap held in all 23 months analysed.
3. **Arrival is the biggest precinct-specific pain point.** Arrival and check-in complaints are **73% higher**
   near Crown (keys, building access, lifts, finding the entrance), and parking complaints are also 73% higher.
4. **Hotel-style stays are judged hardest.** Hotel and serviced stays have the highest complaint rate (8.2%) and
   a lower average rating (4.58 vs 4.79). **Staff complaints are 3.2x more common** than in private homes, and they
   are the most damaging: when a guest complains about staff, the review as a whole turns negative.
5. **Accuracy, cleanliness and value drive the rating; location doesn't.** Together the six category ratings
   explain 89% of the variation in overall ratings. Location is the weakest driver, so a great location does not
   protect a stay that disappoints in other ways.
6. **January is the danger month.** Near Crown, January had the highest complaint rate in both years
   (7.7% in 2025, 9.0% in 2026): summer heat plus Australian Open crowds.

## 2. Which complaints to fix first

Frequency alone is not enough. A complaint matters more when it ruins the whole stay. For each theme I measured
the overall sentiment of the review when that complaint appears (lower = more damaging):

- **Fix first (frequent and damaging): cleanliness.** It is common (13.4 per 1,000) and, after value and staff,
  it does the most damage to the overall review.
- **Rare but severe: staff & hospitality, and value for money.** They don't happen often, but when they do the guest's
  whole review turns sour. In hotel-style stays both push the average review below zero (-0.23 and -0.20).
- **Frequent but tolerated: sleep & noise, room comfort, arrival.** These are the most common complaints but are usually
  mentioned as a "minor downside" in an otherwise positive review. They are worth fixing because of the volume.

## 3. What drives the overall star rating

A regression of each listing's overall rating on its six category ratings (13,280 listings with 10+ reviews, R² = 0.89):

| Category | Impact (standardised) | Average score | Listings below 4.5 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Accuracy | 0.32 | 4.82 | 5.7% |
| **Cleanliness** | 0.26 | **4.75** | **12.3%** |
| Value | 0.23 | 4.74 | 9.9% |
| Communication | 0.14 | 4.88 | 3.3% |
| Check-in | 0.07 | 4.83 | 5.9% |
| Location | 0.05 | 4.87 | 2.1% |

**Reading it:** cleanliness is the clearest opportunity. It is the second-strongest driver, and it has the most
listings falling below 4.5 (one in eight). Superhosts, who meet a consistent service standard, average 4.88 vs 4.71
for other hosts. That suggests consistent standards are worth about +0.17 stars.

## 4. When: seasonality and events

- Guest volume near Crown builds through the **spring racing season (Oct-Dec)**, with November the busiest month.
- **January** is the worst month for complaints in both years. Compared with other months near Crown, January has
  more complaints about value (**+57%**), facilities (**+49%**), room comfort, mostly heat and air conditioning
  (**+44%**), and cleanliness (**+34%**). That points to summer heat, event-period pricing and fast room turnarounds.

## 5. Recommendations

| # | Recommendation | Evidence | How to measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Make arrival effortless in the precinct:** map the arrival journey (car park, entrance, keys, lifts), add wayfinding and pre-arrival messages | Arrival complaints +73%, parking +73% near Crown | Arrival complaints per 1,000 reviews, weekly |
| 2 | **Make cleanliness the first priority:** room-readiness audits, and close the loop on every cleanliness complaint within 24 hours | #2 rating driver, 12.3% of listings below 4.5, high severity | Cleanliness score; share of cleanliness complaints closed within 24h |
| 3 | **Protect sleep in an entertainment precinct:** quiet-floor allocation for families and early flights, bedding standards, noise information at booking | Sleep & noise +33% near Crown, the most frequent complaint in hotel-style stays | Sleep & noise complaint rate |
| 4 | **Make service consistent:** service recovery training, and empower front-line staff to fix issues on the spot | Staff complaints 3.2x higher in hotel-style stays, and the most damaging | Severity of staff complaints; repeat-complaint rate |
| 5 | **Set accurate expectations:** room photos, inclusions and fees that match reality | Accuracy is the #1 rating driver; value complaints are severe | Accuracy and value scores |
| 6 | **Prepare for January:** check air conditioning before summer, extra housekeeping for turnarounds, clear event-period pricing | January complaint rate peaked at 9.0% | January vs annual complaint rate |

## 6. A Voice of Guest program blueprint

How the same method would run as an ongoing program:

1. **Collect:** post-stay surveys, complaints and feedback logs, online reviews (Google, TripAdvisor, booking sites), and social media.
2. **Classify:** score sentiment and tag every comment to a guest-journey stage, using the same 12 themes for every source.
3. **Report monthly (GX scorecard):** complaint rate per 1,000 guests by journey stage, the top 3 themes and their severity,
   the trend vs the 3-month average, and a short list of verbatims for each venue or department.
4. **Alert:** flag a theme when its rate rises above its 3-month average, so issues are caught in weeks, not quarters.
5. **Close the loop:** every complaint has an owner and a response time. Fixes are tracked and checked against the next month's numbers.
6. **Share:** workshops with operations teams to turn the top themes into improvement projects, plus recognition for the teams
   whose scores improve.

## Method and limitations

- 947,165 English reviews cleaned. Sentence-level analysis covers the last 24 months (377,310 reviews, 704,857 theme-tagged sentences).
- Sentiment: VADER, adjusted for hospitality phrasing. Complaint = a sentence scoring -0.05 or lower that mentions a theme.
- **Precision check:** 45 of 60 randomly sampled complaint sentences (75%) were genuine complaints. Treat the rates as comparisons
  between segments and months, not as exact counts.
- Public short-stay reviews are a proxy for hotel guest feedback. They skew positive, and unhappy guests may not review at all.
